# Caller context: live measurements, 2026-10-24

**Bottom line.** The hypothesis was that showing the judge where a changed function is used would remove the "blast radius unknown" holds on correct fixes. **It did not hold on this benchmark.** On a fresh seed (40 cases, each judged twice by live `jev-1.14.0`, without and with `context.callers`), correct fixes to a function with callers were **approved 10 of 10 without the callers and 3 of 10 with them**, because seeing four callers made Jev cite blast radius (5 of 10). The 30 risky cases were blocked **30 of 30 both ways**, so the callers added no catches. Tokens per case rose by a third. Callers do change the explanation: the risky cases now name blast radius (23 of 30 with callers, 0 of 30 without). Because of the cost to approvals, **the feature is opt-in and off by default** (`JEV_MCP_CALLER_CONTEXT=on`, or `jev-mcp ci --callers`). Raw data: [JSON](callers-2026-10-24.json).

Tokens per case: 1.2k without, 1.7k with (+34%). The local "tests are green" rule approved 22 of the 30 risky cases.

## What the cases are

Small diffs in git format with the function's callers listed separately (the same diff both ways): a correct fix that keeps the signature; a new required parameter whose callers still pass the old arguments; a requested change from an empty list to `null` while callers still `.map` the result; and a bad patch (skipped test, swallowed error, hard-coded case) to a function with callers.

## What happened to the design

- **Tuning, on three other seeds.** With the first wording of the instruction, correct fixes fell from 10/10 to 3/10 approved. The final wording, which says that callers which stay valid do not widen the blast radius, gave 6/10 and then 2/10 on two more tuning seeds, and 3/10 on the holdout above. I stopped there instead of tuning until the number looked good.
- **Why I expect this result.** These small diffs were already judged on their own: Jev did not hold the correct fixes for "unknown callers" (0 of 10 cited blast radius without), and it already blocked every risky one. The earlier "blast-radius holds on correct patches" came from larger diffs. Showing callers gives the judge something concrete to weigh, and it weighs it as a reason for care.
- **What is safe.** The snippets are untrusted: sanitized, secrets redacted, dropped if they look like instructions (the pull-request gate then holds with `caller_context_injection`). The decision policy is unchanged and a test checks that identical model answers give an identical decision with and without callers, so callers cannot approve anything by themselves.

## Limits

One model, one run per case, templates written by me, small diffs only. A repository where Jev holds correct changes for unknown callers (the large-diff case) may behave differently; this run does not say. The risky set was already blocked completely without callers, so the benchmark cannot show a catch that callers might add on subtler cases.
